Section A — Fundamentals of Soil & Forensic Significance

1. Soil is considered in forensic science primarily as:
A. Biological evidence
B. Trace/transfer evidence
C. Digital evidence
D. Testimonial evidence
Answer: B — Trace/transfer evidence
Solution: Soil is a classic form of trace evidence because small quantities can be transferred between people, objects and locations. Soil may adhere to shoes, clothing, tyres, tools, bodies and other objects. Its physical, chemical, mineralogical and biological characteristics can then be compared with questioned and known samples.

100. Most important integrated question: Which statement BEST describes forensic soil examination?
A. Soil can uniquely identify a person in every case
B. Soil comparison determines whether questioned and known soils could share a common origin by examining multiple physical, chemical, mineralogical and biological characteristics
C. Soil analysis is limited to colour comparison
D. Soil is not transferable evidence
Answer: B
Solution: This is the core concept you should remember for an FSL examination.
Soil is a complex, heterogeneous and transferable trace material. Its forensic examination may involve:
  • colour and morphology,
  • texture,
  • particle-size distribution,
  • density characteristics,
  • mineralogy,
  • pH,
  • organic matter,
  • microscopy,
  • XRD, FTIR/ATR-FTIR, SEM-EDX, ICP-MS/ICP-OES,
  • biological components such as plant material, pollen and spores,
  • and, where appropriate, statistical/multivariate analysis.
The objective is generally to compare questioned soil with known/reference samples and assess whether a common origin is supported or excluded, rather than automatically “identifying” a person.

🔥 25 Key Facts You Should Memorise for UPSSSC FSL

  1. Soil = trace/transfer evidence.
  2. Locard's exchange principle explains soil transfer.
  3. Soil may transfer to shoes, clothing, tyres and tools.
  4. Soil is a complex mixture of physical + chemical + mineralogical + biological components.
  5. Pedology = study of soil as a natural body.
  6. Texture mainly concerns sand, silt and clay.
  7. Clay < silt < sand in particle size.
  8. Soil colour is a valuable screening characteristic.
  9. Organic matter commonly contributes to dark soil colour.
  10. Iron compounds strongly influence red/yellow/brown soil colours.
  11. Soil can vary laterally and vertically.
  12. Collect samples as soon as possible.
  13. Collect from the crime scene + relevant access/escape routes.
  14. Consider depth when depth is relevant to questioned soil.
  15. Do not remove adhering soil from shoes/clothing/tools before examination.
  16. Use representative known/control samples.
  17. Density-gradient methods are used in soil comparison.
  18. XRD = mineralogical/crystalline phase analysis.
  19. SEM = morphology.
  20. EDX/EDS = elemental composition.
  21. FTIR = molecular/chemical characterisation.
  22. ICP-MS/ICP-OES = elemental analysis.
  23. Clay minerals such as kaolinite and montmorillonite can be useful for discrimination.
  24. Multiple complementary characteristics are generally stronger than one characteristic.
  25. The normal forensic conclusion concerns association/common origin or exclusion, not automatic proof of guilt.
